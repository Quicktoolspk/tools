Dustin May Joins Chicago White Sox on $52.5 Million Deal

November 17, 2026 6 min read By QuickToolsPK Sports

The Chicago White Sox have agreed to sign right-handed pitcher Dustin May to a three-year, $52.5 million contract, making a major move to strengthen their starting rotation ahead of the 2027 MLB season.

The agreement was confirmed by May's agent, Craig Rose, and is pending a physical. The deal brings the 29-year-old pitcher to Chicago after he spent the 2026 season with the St. Louis Cardinals and Milwaukee Brewers.

The signing gives the White Sox an experienced starting pitcher with a powerful fastball and postseason experience. However, May's injury history and inconsistent results also make this an interesting investment for the club.

Why Did the White Sox Sign Dustin May?

Starting pitching was one of Chicago's biggest priorities entering the offseason. The team needed more dependable innings and additional experience in its rotation. May offers several qualities that make him an appealing addition:

  • High-velocity fastball: May has regularly thrown a fastball in the upper 90s.
  • Starting experience: He has experience handling a regular starting rotation workload.
  • Postseason background: He has appeared in major playoff games and won two World Series championships.
  • Age and potential: At 29, he still has time to improve and deliver stronger seasons.

Dustin May's Injury History

One of the biggest questions surrounding May is his health. During his career with the Los Angeles Dodgers, he experienced significant injury setbacks. He underwent Tommy John revision surgery and missed the entire 2024 season while recovering. He also suffered an esophageal tear.

His workload has increased over the last two seasons. He pitched 132⅓ innings in 2025 and reached 145⅔ innings in 2026. That progress is encouraging, but maintaining his health over a three-year contract will be important for Chicago.
