Florida Learjet Crash: Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, Upper Jets, Video & Who Was on Board

Updated: November 2, 2026 9 min read ● Investigation Ongoing
Breaking: A Bombardier Learjet 60 crashed in Greenacres, Florida on November 1, 2026. All 9 people aboard died. The NTSB is investigating.

Emergency response at the Greenacres, Florida crash site on November 1, 2026. (Illustrative image — replace with editorially licensed photograph)

A private Bombardier Learjet 60 crashed in Greenacres, Palm Beach County, Florida, on Sunday, November 1, 2026, killing all nine people aboard. The aircraft had departed from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was travelling to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas when it went down near South Military Trail.

The crash happened at approximately 12:50 p.m. local time. The aircraft struck the ground near a residential community and a busy roadway, creating a large fireball, thick black smoke and a significant emergency response.

One of the victims was identified by her family as 20-year-old co-pilot Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, a young aviator from South Florida who had earned her private pilot's licence at just 17.

The crash has attracted widespread attention because of the aircraft's dramatic descent, dashcam footage showing the impact, emergency radio communications and questions about the people aboard the flight.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirmed that nine people were aboard and said the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) would lead the investigation. The official cause of the crash has not yet been established.

Who Was Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras?

Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras was a 20-year-old aspiring commercial airline pilot from the Boca Raton area of Palm Beach County, Florida. Her family confirmed that she was serving as the co-pilot aboard the Learjet 60 when it crashed in Greenacres.

Her story has drawn attention because she achieved an important aviation milestone while still in high school. In 2023, at the age of 17, she earned her private pilot's licence before obtaining her driver's licence.

According to local reporting, Nathalia began ground-school training in 2020 while attending West Boca Raton High School. She also learned about aviation from her father, who had started flying recreationally in 2018.

A 2023 television interview highlighted her unusual achievement and her enthusiasm for flying. At that point, she had reportedly accumulated 88 flight hours while working towards her private pilot's licence.

Her ambition was to build a career in aviation and become a commercial airline pilot. Reports published after the crash described her as an aspiring professional pilot who had continued developing her aviation experience.

What Happened to the Learjet in Florida?

The aircraft was travelling from Austin, Texas, towards the Bahamas when its crew began reporting difficult weather conditions during the flight.

According to reporting based on air traffic control communications, the crew reported icing, moderate turbulence and precipitation. The pilot requested a higher altitude in an attempt to find more favourable conditions.

The situation then deteriorated rapidly. The crew declared an emergency and reported that the aircraft was descending at an extremely high rate. Communications included warnings about an overspeed condition and the possibility of striking the ground.

Flight-tracking information reported by news organisations showed a dramatic loss of altitude shortly before the aircraft disappeared from tracking data.

The Learjet eventually crashed near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in Greenacres. The impact caused a major fire and left a crater and burning debris near homes and businesses.

Authorities reported that all nine people aboard died. No immediate deaths or injuries on the ground were reported.

What Was Heard in the Learjet's Final Radio Communications?

One of the most widely discussed parts of the accident is the air traffic control audio recorded during the aircraft's final moments.

Reports based on the communications say the crew initially described icing and moderate turbulence. They then requested a higher altitude.

As the situation became more serious, the crew declared an emergency and reported a rapid descent. The communications included a warning that the aircraft was descending at approximately 10,000 feet per minute, followed by reports of an overspeed condition and an even faster descent.

The recordings have drawn attention because they provide information about the crew's awareness of the emergency and the rapidly changing flight conditions.

Editorial caution: Some media reports have described a female voice associated with Nathalia and an exchange involving someone addressed by the name Jack or Jeff. The published accounts are not consistent about the name, and the identity of the other person has not been officially established. It would be misleading to identify that individual as her boyfriend, partner or another particular person.

What Is Upper Jets? How Is It Connected?

Upper Jets has been mentioned in coverage of the Florida Learjet crash because of its reported connection to the aircraft and the aviation work of Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras.

The Guardian reported that the aircraft was owned by a Fort Lauderdale charter company, Upper Jets, while other reporting described Nathalia as working as a pilot for the company.

It is important to distinguish the different roles involved in private aviation:

  • Aircraft owner: The individual or legal entity that owns or holds title to the aircraft.
  • Charter operator: The company responsible for arranging or operating charter flights, depending on its regulatory authority and contractual arrangements.
  • Flight crew: The pilots and other crew members operating the aircraft.
  • Registered owner: The person or company recorded in the aircraft registration records.

Early reporting has also described the aircraft as registered to a Delaware-based LLC. The precise ownership, operating arrangements and responsibilities should be established from official records and the investigation rather than inferred from a company name appearing in news coverage.

At this stage, the fact that Upper Jets has been mentioned in connection with the flight does not establish that the company caused or contributed to the crash. No such conclusion should be published without official findings.

The aircraft crashed in Greenacres, a city in Palm Beach County, South Florida.

The crash caused a major emergency response. Authorities closed the affected roadway and evacuated approximately 600 residents from a nearby mobile-home community while officials dealt with the scene and the investigation.

The location of the accident made the incident particularly alarming because the aircraft came down near homes and a busy road. Officials reported no immediate deaths or injuries among people on the ground.

What Caused the Florida Learjet Crash?

The exact cause of the crash has not been officially determined. However, early reports have identified several areas that investigators may examine.

Severe Weather

The crew reported precipitation, icing and moderate turbulence. Weather may be relevant but its role has not been established.

Aircraft Icing

The crew reportedly described ice accumulating on the aircraft. Ice can affect aerodynamics and control.

Rapid Loss of Altitude

Recordings and tracking data described an extremely fast descent shortly before impact.

Aircraft Condition

Maintenance records, inspections and any mechanical issues will be examined by investigators.

Crew Decisions

Flight planning, weather information and crew actions are standard areas of focus.

Important: None of these possibilities should be described as the confirmed cause. Only the NTSB's investigation can establish what happened.

What Is a Learjet 60?

The Learjet 60 is a business jet developed by Learjet, a company whose aircraft programme became part of Bombardier's business aviation portfolio.

It is designed for private and corporate travel rather than ordinary scheduled airline services. The aircraft belongs to the Learjet 60 family and is known as a midsize business jet.

Private business jets are commonly used for executive travel, charter operations and journeys between airports that may not be served by direct commercial flights.

In the Florida accident, the FAA identified the aircraft as a Learjet 60. The aircraft's specific technical condition and flight history are matters for the investigators to examine.

What Happens Next in the NTSB Investigation?

The National Transportation Safety Board is responsible for leading the investigation, with assistance from the FAA.

1 Wreckage examination: Investigators document the impact site and inspect aircraft components.
2 Flight data analysis: Available flight-tracking information and recorded data are examined.
3 Communication review: Air traffic control recordings and other communications are analysed.
4 Weather assessment: Meteorological information is reviewed to understand conditions along the flight path.
5 Maintenance review: Aircraft records and maintenance history are examined.
6 Final report: Investigators publish findings and identify contributing factors, if established.

The investigation may take considerable time. Early news reports should not be mistaken for a final NTSB conclusion. The FAA has specifically stated that the NTSB will lead this investigation and provide updates.
