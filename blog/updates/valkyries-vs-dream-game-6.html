Valkyries vs Dream Game 6: Score, Result, Where to Watch and WNBA Finals Updates

Last checked: October 29, 2026 5 min read Live / Developing Story

Golden State Valkyries and Atlanta Dream face off in a pivotal Game 6. (Illustrative image)

The Golden State Valkyries and Atlanta Dream are facing off in a pivotal Game 6 of the 2026 WNBA Finals. Golden State entered the matchup with a 3–2 series lead after winning Games 4 and 5, putting the franchise one victory away from its first WNBA championship. Atlanta, meanwhile, needed a home-court win to keep its title hopes alive and force a decisive Game 7.

The matchup has drawn attention from basketball fans searching for the Valkyries vs Dream score, where to watch the WNBA Finals, Allisha Gray's performance, and whether the series will go to a seventh game.

Game 6 was scheduled for Thursday, October 29, at State Farm Arena in Atlanta, with a 12:30 p.m. Eastern Time tipoff. The WNBA moved the game from its originally scheduled evening slot to accommodate the Atlanta Hawks' NBA game at the same arena later that night.

Why Game 6 matters for the WNBA Finals

Game 6 was a potential championship-clinching game for Golden State. The Valkyries had won two consecutive games after Atlanta took a 2–1 lead, changing the momentum of the series.

The stakes were straightforward:

  • If Golden State won: the Valkyries would claim the first championship in franchise history.
  • If Atlanta won: the series would be tied 3–3, sending the Finals to Game 7 in San Francisco on Saturday, October 31, at 8 p.m. ET, according to the announced schedule.

That difference explains why searches for "WNBA Game 7," "WNBA Finals Game 6," and "Valkyries game" are closely related. Fans are looking not only for the score but also for the championship outcome.

Why did Game 6 start so early?

The game was moved to 12:30 p.m. ET because State Farm Arena also had an Atlanta Hawks NBA game scheduled for that evening. Keeping the Finals at the larger venue allowed more fans to attend than the Dream's smaller regular-season arena could accommodate.

The early tipoff meant the game began at 9:30 a.m. Pacific Time, an unusual start for a WNBA Finals matchup. The schedule change became a talking point among fans because many viewers on the West Coast needed to watch during the workday.

How did the teams reach Game 6?

Golden State and Atlanta traded wins through the first five games. The Dream initially took control of the series, but the Valkyries responded with back-to-back victories to move ahead 3–2.
