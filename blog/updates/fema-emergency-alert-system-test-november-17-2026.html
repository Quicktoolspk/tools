FEMA Nationwide Emergency Alert System Test: November 17, 2026 Recap

November 17, 2026 4 min read By QuickToolsPK News

Did you notice a sudden interruption in your television or radio broadcast? On Tuesday, November 17, 2026, the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) carried out a scheduled, mandatory nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System (EAS).

Direct Answer: Yes, the nationwide EAS test took place on November 17, 2026, at approximately 2:20 p.m. ET (1:20 p.m. CT). The test interrupted normal radio and television broadcasts for about one minute to verify the reliability of the national public warning infrastructure.

By law, FEMA is required to conduct a nationwide test of public alert and warning systems at least once every three years to identify and resolve any technical or operational flaws before a real crisis occurs.

What Happened During the Broadcast Test?

Unlike wireless emergency alerts sent directly to mobile phones, the November 17 test targeted traditional broadcast and cable distribution channels. Radio and television broadcasters, cable systems, satellite radio and television providers, and wireline video providers temporarily paused normal programming to relay the test.

"This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency, covering the United States from 2:20 to 2:50 hours ET. This is only a test. No action is required by the public."

The test included standard EAS alert tones designed to support individuals who are deaf or hard of hearing, and participating stations broadcasted in English or Spanish depending on local programming standards. The interruption lasted approximately one minute, after which regular broadcasting immediately resumed.

Which Systems Were Included (and Excluded)?

It is important to understand exactly what this test covered and what it did not.

Included in Test

  • Television Broadcasters: Local and national TV channels.
  • Radio Stations: AM, FM, and satellite radio.
  • Cable & Wireline: Cable providers and streaming video subscription services tied to wireline infrastructure.

Excluded from Test

  • Cell Phones: Standard cellular emergency push alerts (WEA) were not part of this specific exercise.
  • NOAA Weather Radios: These devices did not receive this specific EAS broadcast.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did I need to do anything when the emergency alert went off?
No. The public was instructed that no action was required. It was strictly a routine, scheduled test of the nation's broadcast warning infrastructure.
Why did FEMA run this test on November 17, 2026?
FEMA is federally mandated to test the Emergency Alert System at least once every three years. These periodic checks ensure that broadcast pathways can reliably relay emergency warnings during severe weather, national security threats, or other major crises.
Did cell phones get an emergency alert test on November 17?
No. The November 17 test was specifically targeted toward television and radio broadcasters, cable operators, and satellite providers rather than Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) on mobile phones.
What happens if a broadcaster fails to air the test?
Participating broadcast and cable providers are legally required by the FCC to relay national EAS test messages. Non-compliance can result in regulatory reviews or penalties by the Federal Communications Commission.
What is the backup date if the test had failed?
In the event of unforeseen technical issues or major live emergencies pre-empting the schedule, FEMA established a designated backup test date of Thursday, December 3, 2026. However, the primary November 17 test proceeded as scheduled.

Conclusion

The nationwide EAS test on November 17, 2026, was a successful exercise in ensuring the reliability of America's public warning infrastructure. While it briefly interrupted television and radio broadcasts, the test required no action from the public and serves as a vital safeguard for future emergencies.
