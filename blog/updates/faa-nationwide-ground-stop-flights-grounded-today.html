FAA Nationwide Ground Stop: Why Are Flights Grounded Today?

Air travel across the United States faced a major disruption on Friday, November 13, 2026, after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a rare nationwide ground stop for departing flights. The decision followed communication problems affecting air traffic control centers responsible for managing aircraft traveling between airports.

The FAA instructed airlines and aircraft operators to keep departing flights on the ground until further notice, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety concerns. The disruption affected airports across the country, leaving passengers facing delays, cancellations, missed connections, and uncertainty about when their flights would depart.

But what exactly is an FAA ground stop? Why are flights grounded, and does a nationwide ground stop mean every airplane must land immediately?

What Happened With the FAA Nationwide Ground Stop on November 13, 2026?

On November 13, 2026, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop affecting departing flights at airports across the United States.

According to the Associated Press, communication problems affected 22 regional Air Route Traffic Control Centers. These centers help manage aircraft traveling through the country's airspace between airports.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said FAA technicians were investigating the communication issue. The agency ordered the ground stop because of safety concerns while the problem was being addressed.

The FAA advisory instructed aircraft operators to keep departing traffic on the ground until further notice. It also stated that inbound international flights would be managed on a case-by-case basis.

Did the FAA ground all airplanes?

No. The order primarily stopped departing flights from taking off.

Aircraft that were already airborne were not automatically ordered to land immediately. Officials said flights already in the air could land safely, while inbound international flights would be handled individually.

This distinction matters because a nationwide ground stop does not necessarily mean every airport is closed or every aircraft is forced to land.

Why were flights grounded across the United States?

The reported reason was a communication problem affecting air traffic control centers. These centers coordinate aircraft moving through different regions of the national airspace. When controllers cannot reliably communicate or coordinate aircraft movements, continuing normal departures can create safety risks.

The FAA therefore ordered departing traffic to remain on the ground while technicians investigated the issue. Officials had not confirmed a specific technical root cause in the reports available at publication time.

It is important not to confuse a reported communications problem with a confirmed cyberattack, software failure, or specific equipment malfunction. Those explanations should not be treated as established facts unless authorities confirm them.

What Is an FAA Ground Stop?

An FAA ground stop is an air traffic management restriction that temporarily prevents certain aircraft from departing.

The restriction can apply to:

  • A single airport.
  • Several airports.
  • A particular region or area of airspace.
  • Specific aircraft operations.
  • A wider network of airports, as in a nationwide ground stop.

During a ground stop, affected aircraft generally remain at their departure airports until the restriction is lifted or updated.

The FAA uses ground stops to manage situations where normal air traffic operations cannot safely continue. These situations may involve reduced airport capacity, severe weather, equipment problems, or other operational concerns.

A ground stop is different from a normal flight delay because it is an operational restriction issued through the air traffic control system rather than simply an airline scheduling issue.

What Causes Flights to Be Grounded?

A ground stop can happen for several reasons. Not every ground stop is caused by a nationwide technical problem.

1. Air Traffic Control Communication Problems

Air traffic controllers need reliable communication systems to coordinate aircraft movements. If communication between control facilities becomes unreliable, controllers may not be able to manage normal traffic safely. In such circumstances, the FAA may restrict departures until the issue is resolved.

2. Severe Weather Conditions

Thunderstorms, hurricanes, snowstorms, strong winds, and low visibility can disrupt flight operations. Bad weather can reduce the number of aircraft an airport can safely handle. It can also affect runways, visibility, arrival routes, and departure procedures.

3. Airport Equipment Problems

Airports and air traffic control facilities depend on equipment such as radar systems, communication networks, navigation aids, and runway lighting. When critical equipment stops working, normal operations may need to be restricted until technicians resolve the problem.

4. Runway Closures and Airport Capacity

An airport may temporarily lose access to one or more runways because of maintenance, an aircraft incident, debris, or other operational issues. A reduction in runway capacity can create a backlog of departing and arriving aircraft.

5. Security or Safety Concerns

Airports and aviation authorities may restrict flight operations when they identify a potential safety or security concern. A ground stop can provide time to assess the situation, coordinate with relevant agencies, and determine when normal operations can resume.

Ground Stop vs. Ground Delay vs. Flight Cancellation

These terms are often confused, especially when passengers see several different updates on airline apps. However, they describe different situations.

TermWhat it means
Ground StopA restriction that temporarily prevents affected aircraft from departing.
Ground DelayA flight is delayed before departure, often because of traffic management or airport capacity restrictions.
Flight CancellationAn airline removes a scheduled flight from operation.
Airport ClosureAn airport temporarily suspends some or all airport operations.
Airspace RestrictionAircraft operations are restricted in a specified area of airspace.

A ground stop does not automatically cancel every affected flight. Some flights may depart once restrictions are lifted, while others may be delayed further or canceled because of scheduling and operational complications.

How Long Will the FAA Ground Stop Last?

There is no fixed duration for an FAA ground stop. Some restrictions last only a short time, while others may continue for several hours or longer. The duration depends on the cause, the severity of the disruption, and how quickly normal operations can safely resume.

For example, the previous nationwide ground stop in January 2023 followed a failure in an FAA pilot notification system and lasted less than two hours, according to Reuters. The November 13, 2026, incident involved a different reported issue, so the earlier event cannot be used to predict when the current disruption will end.

Even after the FAA lifts a ground stop, flights may not return to normal immediately.

Airlines may still need to:

  • Reposition aircraft and crews.
  • Reassign passengers to available flights.
  • Resolve missed connections.
  • Clear airport departure queues.
  • Update departure and arrival schedules.

For passengers, this means that a flight may remain delayed even after the original restriction has ended.

Are flights canceled or just delayed?

A nationwide ground stop can cause both delays and cancellations, but they are not the same thing. When the FAA restricts departures, airlines may initially hold flights at their departure airports. If the restriction continues, the airline may need to adjust its schedule.

Some flights may operate later than planned. Others may be canceled if aircraft or crew members cannot reach the right locations, passengers miss connecting flights, or the airline cannot maintain its schedule.

Passengers should check their individual flight status instead of assuming that every flight has been canceled.

How to Check If Your Flight Is Grounded or Delayed

If you are traveling today, use official flight information rather than relying only on social media posts or general news headlines.

1. Check your airline's flight status page

Visit your airline's official website or mobile app. Search using your flight number or route to see whether your flight is on time, delayed, canceled, boarding, departed, or diverted.

Airline information is especially important because the FAA's nationwide announcement does not tell you the exact status of every individual booking.

2. Check the FAA National Airspace System Status

The FAA provides air traffic information through its National Airspace System Status page. Passengers can use it to review operational advisories, airport delays, ground stops, and other air traffic updates.

Official resource: FAA National Airspace System Status

The information can change as the FAA updates its advisories, so check again before leaving for the airport.

3. Use an independent flight tracking service

Flight tracking platforms can help you follow an aircraft's movement and review delays or cancellations. Examples include FlightAware and Flightradar24.

These services can provide useful additional information, but always confirm important changes with your airline.

4. Check your airport's official website

Airport websites may publish terminal announcements, departure information, security updates, and other passenger notices. If you are already at the airport, also check the departure screens and listen for announcements from airline staff.

What Should Passengers Do During a Nationwide Ground Stop?

A nationwide flight disruption can be stressful, particularly when you have connecting flights, hotel bookings, business meetings, or important appointments. The following steps can help you manage the situation.

Don't leave for the airport without checking your flight

Before traveling to the airport, check your airline's app or website. A flight may be delayed or canceled even if the airport itself remains open.

Contact your airline about rebooking

If your flight is canceled or significantly delayed, contact the airline to understand your available options. Depending on its policies and the circumstances, the airline may offer rebooking or other assistance.

You can also check whether changes are available through the airline's website or mobile app rather than waiting in a long airport customer-service line.

Keep your booking details accessible

Keep your confirmation email, ticket number, flight number, and identification documents available. These details can make it easier to communicate with airline representatives and review alternative travel arrangements.

Review connecting flights

If you have a connecting flight, check both segments of your journey. A delay on your first flight may cause you to miss the next departure, even if the connecting airport is operating normally.

Save receipts for additional expenses

Keep receipts for meals, transportation, accommodation, and other expenses related to the disruption. Whether an airline reimburses these costs depends on its policies, the reason for the disruption, and applicable passenger protections.

Is a Nationwide Ground Stop the Same as an Airport Shutdown?

No. A nationwide ground stop and an airport shutdown are different.

A ground stop restricts aircraft departures under specified air traffic instructions. An airport shutdown means the airport has suspended some or all of its operations.

During a ground stop, airports may remain open to passengers and airport employees. Shops, restaurants, security checkpoints, and other facilities may continue operating. However, the airport can become crowded as departing passengers wait for updates and arriving aircraft continue to reach their destinations.

Why Does Air Traffic Control Communication Matter So Much?

Air traffic control is essential for keeping aircraft separated and coordinating safe movement through the national airspace. Controllers communicate with pilots and coordinate with other control facilities as aircraft travel between airports.

Regional Air Route Traffic Control Centers manage aircraft during the en-route portion of a journey. Their work helps ensure that aircraft follow assigned routes and maintain safe separation from other traffic.

When communication or coordination systems are disrupted, controllers may have limited ability to manage normal traffic volumes. That is why aviation authorities may choose to stop departures rather than allow additional aircraft to enter an already affected system.

Frequently Asked Questions About the FAA Ground Stop

1. Why are flights grounded in the United States today?
On November 13, 2026, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for departing flights after communication problems affected regional air traffic control centers. The agency cited safety concerns while technicians investigated the issue.

An FAA ground stop is an air traffic restriction that temporarily prevents affected aircraft from departing. It may apply to one airport, several airports, a region, or the entire country.

No. A ground stop does not automatically cancel all flights. Departures may be held temporarily, while some flights may operate later and others may be canceled because of continuing operational disruption.
4. Can planes already in the air land during a ground stop?
Yes. During the November 13, 2026, nationwide ground stop, reports said aircraft already airborne could continue to land. The order focused on stopping departing traffic.
5. How long do FAA ground stops usually last?
There is no standard duration. A ground stop may last minutes or several hours, depending on the problem and when safe operations can resume.

Check your airline's official website or mobile app using your flight number. You can also review the FAA's National Airspace System Status page and use a flight tracking service for additional information.
7. Does a ground stop mean the airport is closed?
No. An airport can remain open while departing flights are temporarily restricted. Airport closures and FAA ground stops are different operational situations.

Final Thoughts

The FAA nationwide ground stop on November 13, 2026, demonstrated how an air traffic control communication problem can disrupt departing flights across the United States.

A ground stop is a safety measure, not automatically a declaration that every airport is closed or every flight has been canceled. Aircraft already in the air may continue to land, while departing flights remain on the ground until restrictions change.

For travelers, the most important thing is to stay informed. Check your airline's flight status, follow official FAA updates, and confirm any rebooking arrangements directly with your airline.
