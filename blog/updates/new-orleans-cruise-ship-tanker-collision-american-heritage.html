New Orleans Cruise Ship Crash: Tanker Collides With Docked American Heritage Riverboat

Last updated: November 13, 2026 8 min read Developing Story
Breaking News
Tanker collides with docked American Heritage cruise ship

A major multi-agency water rescue is underway on the Mississippi River near the Port of New Orleans. River traffic is closed between mile markers 93 and 96.

Emergency responders launched a massive water rescue on the Mississippi River after a tanker struck the American Heritage riverboat. (Illustrative image)

A major maritime emergency unfolded along the New Orleans riverfront on Friday afternoon, November 13, 2026, when a commercial asphalt tanker slammed into a docked luxury river cruise ship on the Mississippi River. The impact forced the five-story paddlewheel riverboat American Heritage to roll onto its side and partially submerge, triggering a multi-agency water rescue.

Direct Answer: At approximately 12:30 p.m. CT on November 13, 2026, the Marshall Islands-flagged tanker Atlantic Asphalt collided with the docked American Cruise Lines riverboat American Heritage near the Port of New Orleans. Roughly 129 to 130 passengers and crew were aboard; emergency crews rescued multiple people from the water, transported dozens to local hospitals, and closed a three-mile stretch of the Mississippi River to vessel traffic.

Verified Timeline of the November 13, 2026 Collision

The incident took place along a busy stretch of the New Orleans riverfront near the Port of New Orleans Administration Building, the Ernest N. Morial Convention Center, and the Crescent City Connection bridge.

Before 12:30 p.m. CT – Vessel Docked

The American Heritage, operated by American Cruise Lines, was moored at the Port of New Orleans near mile marker 96 at the conclusion of an eight-day Mississippi River itinerary.

~12:30 p.m. CT – The Impact

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt veered toward the riverbank and struck the docked American Heritage. Eyewitnesses described the sound of "tons of steel crunching" as the riverboat was pushed against the dock.

12:30 p.m. to 12:45 p.m. CT – Roll-Over and Evacuation

Within roughly 15 minutes of the initial collision, the American Heritage took on water, rolled onto its side toward the river, and became partially submerged. Passengers climbed over the railings as crew members distributed life jackets.

Afternoon Response – Multi-Agency Rescue

Boats from the U.S. Coast Guard (USCG), New Orleans Fire Department (NOFD), New Orleans Police Department (NOPD), Port NOLA Harbor Police, and nearby tugboats converged on the scene to pull passengers wearing life jackets out of the fast-moving river current.

Post-Collision Closures

By mid-afternoon, the U.S. Coast Guard shut down all vessel traffic between Mississippi River mile markers 93 and 96, while the New Orleans Regional Transit Authority (RTA) suspended cross-river ferry service as a safety precaution.

Passenger Rescues, Injuries, and Official Response

Emergency officials prioritized accounting for everyone aboard the American Heritage and treating injured passengers.

  • Hospitalizations: Local media and emergency medical officials reported that roughly two dozen to 32 passengers were transported to area hospitals for evaluation and treatment. Uninjured passengers and crew were escorted to the Port of New Orleans Administration Building for shelter and assistance.
  • Statement from American Cruise Lines: The cruise operator confirmed that guests and crew were evacuated from the vessel, stating: "We are in close coordination with the U.S. Coast Guard and local authorities. Guests and crew were evacuated, and we are deeply concerned for everyone involved."
  • State and City Leadership: Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry traveled to New Orleans to coordinate state support alongside New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno, while the U.S. Department of Homeland Security confirmed Coast Guard assets were leading the maritime response.

What Caused the Tanker to Hit the Cruise Ship?

As of the latest official briefings, the U.S. Coast Guard and National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) have not yet released a formal determination of the root cause.

However, an emergency response source indicated to media outlets that investigators are examining whether the Atlantic Asphalt suffered a sudden loss of power or steering/mechanical failure in the strong currents of the Lower Mississippi River prior to striking the moored riverboat.

Officials have not confirmed whether the tanker was carrying cargo at the time of impact or whether any fuel or asphalt leaked into the river.

How the Crash Affects New Orleans Travel and River Cruises

If you live in the New Orleans area or have upcoming travel plans through the Port of New Orleans, keep the following operational impacts in mind:

  • Mississippi River Traffic Closure: The U.S. Coast Guard closed the Mississippi River to all commercial and recreational vessels between mile markers 93 and 96 while salvage and response crews secure the partially submerged riverboat.
  • Suspended Local Ferry Service: The New Orleans Regional Transit Authority temporarily halted cross-river pedestrian and vehicle ferry operations. Commuters should use the Crescent City Connection bridge and allow extra travel time.
  • Avoid the Riverfront Area: New Orleans' emergency preparedness agency (NOLA Ready) urged residents and tourists to stay clear of the riverfront upriver of the French Quarter near the Convention Center so emergency vehicles can operate freely.
  • Upcoming Cruise Itineraries: Travelers booked on upcoming American Heritage sailings—or other river cruises departing from the Port of New Orleans—should contact their cruise line directly for itinerary modifications, rebooking options, or refund policies. Quick Summary

  • The Incident: On November 13, 2026, at ~12:30 p.m. CT, the tanker Atlantic Asphalt collided with the docked American Heritage cruise ship near Port of New Orleans mile marker 96.
  • The Damage: The five-story paddlewheel riverboat rolled onto its side and partially sank within about 15 minutes of being struck.
  • Rescue & Injuries: Roughly 129–130 guests and crew were onboard; emergency boats pulled passengers from the Mississippi River, and up to 32 people were taken to local hospitals.
  • Current Restrictions: The U.S. Coast Guard closed the Mississippi River from mile markers 93 to 96, and local RTA ferry services were temporarily suspended.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Which cruise ship was hit in New Orleans on November 13, 2026?
The vessel struck was the American Heritage, a 150-passenger, five-story paddlewheel riverboat built in 2015 and operated by American Cruise Lines. It was docked near the Port of New Orleans Administration Building at the time of the crash.
2. What ship crashed into the American Heritage riverboat?
The riverboat was struck by the Atlantic Asphalt, a commercial tanker designed to carry asphalt and bitumen. According to maritime tracking data, the tanker is flagged out of the Marshall Islands and had arrived in New Orleans earlier that week from New Jersey.
3. Were there any fatalities in the New Orleans cruise ship crash?
Emergency officials confirmed that dozens of passengers were rescued from the water and up to 32 people were hospitalized. Authorities had not confirmed any fatalities in initial afternoon briefings as search, rescue, and vessel accountability checks continued.
4. Why did the tanker collide with the docked cruise ship?
The official investigation led by the U.S. Coast Guard is ongoing. Preliminary reports from emergency response sources suggest the tanker may have experienced a power or mechanical failure while navigating the Mississippi River.
5. Is the Mississippi River closed in New Orleans right now?
Yes. Following the collision, the U.S. Coast Guard closed the Mississippi River to all vessel traffic between mile markers 93 and 96, and the Regional Transit Authority suspended local ferry service.

Conclusion

The November 13, 2026 collision between the Atlantic Asphalt and the American Heritage highlights how unforgiving the strong currents of the Lower Mississippi River can be when a large commercial vessel loses control near a busy passenger terminal.

Thanks to rapid coordination between the U.S. Coast Guard, New Orleans firefighters, harbor police, and nearby tugboat crews, dozens of passengers were quickly pulled to safety. If you are traveling through New Orleans or scheduled for an upcoming river cruise, monitor official Port of New Orleans and Coast Guard advisories for updates on river reopenings and vessel schedules.

This article reflects reporting available on November 13, 2026. The exact cause of the collision and any final findings about responsibility should be based on official investigation results, not speculation.
