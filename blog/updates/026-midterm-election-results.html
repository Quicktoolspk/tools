2026 Midterm Elections: What Is Happening Today?

Millions of Americans are voting in the 2026 United States midterm elections on Tuesday, November 3. The elections will determine the balance of power in Congress, decide dozens of gubernatorial contests and shape political leadership across the country.

All 435 seats in the US House of Representatives are being contested, along with 35 seats in the US Senate. Voters in 36 states are also choosing governors. In addition, state and local elections and ballot measures are appearing on ballots around the country.

The main national question is whether Republicans will retain control of Congress or Democrats will gain enough seats to take control of one or both chambers.

President Donald Trump is not running for president in this election. However, his administration, policies and approval ratings are major issues in the political debate. Midterm elections often act as a public assessment of the party holding the White House.

More than 59 million Americans had already voted before Election Day, according to reporting citing the University of Florida Election Lab.

What Are the 2026 Midterm Elections?

Midterm elections are nationwide elections held halfway through a US president's four-year term. They take place every two years after presidential elections, meaning the 2026 midterms fall between the 2024 and 2028 presidential elections.

Unlike a presidential election, voters are not choosing a new president in the 2026 midterms. Instead, they are electing members of Congress, governors, state legislators and other public officials.

The results matter because Congress makes federal laws, approves government spending, conducts oversight of the executive branch and votes on important national policies.

The party that controls Congress can have a major influence on the president's legislative agenda.

Republican Party vs Democratic Party: What Is at Stake?

The two main political parties competing in the 2026 midterms are the Republican Party and the Democratic Party.

Republican Party

Often associated with conservative policies, lower taxes, limited federal regulation, stronger border enforcement and a larger role for states in some policy areas.

Democratic Party

Often associated with progressive or liberal policies, expanded public services, environmental protections, reproductive rights and stronger federal action in some areas.

These are broad political descriptions, not complete summaries of every candidate. Individual candidates can have different positions, even within the same party.

Why Are Republicans and Democrats Competing for Congress?

Before Election Day, Republicans held a 53–47 advantage in the Senate. The House was closely divided, with Republicans holding a narrow working majority.

Democrats are seeking to win enough seats to take control of Congress. Republicans are trying to defend their existing majorities.

The House majority threshold is 218 seats. Senate control is more complicated: Republicans can retain effective control with 50 seats because the vice president can break a tie, while Democrats need 51 seats to control the chamber independently.

The Associated Press reported that the election outcome could take days to determine.

US House of Representatives Elections 2026

All 435 voting seats in the House are up for election. Every congressional district across the 50 states has a House contest.

A party needs at least 218 seats to hold a majority. Even a relatively small number of competitive districts can therefore determine which party controls the chamber.

House results may take longer to become clear than some Senate contests because hundreds of individual districts are reporting results separately. Close races, mail ballots and state counting procedures can delay calls.

Governor Elections 2026

Voters in 36 states are choosing governors. Governors lead state executive branches and have responsibilities that include state budgets, public safety, education policy and implementing state laws.

The outcome of gubernatorial elections can influence state-level policy even when the same party does not control the federal government.

Who Is Winning the 2026 Midterm Elections Right Now?

This is one of the most searched questions during Election Day, but the answer depends on which races have reported enough votes to make a reliable assessment.

There is no single national popular vote that determines the winner of the midterms. Senate, House and governor contests are decided separately.

A candidate can be leading in the reported vote while a large number of ballots remain uncounted. A lead is not the same as a confirmed win.

Key Terms You Should Know

Leading Candidate currently has more reported votes.
Projected winner A news organisation believes the remaining vote cannot change the outcome.
Race called A results organisation has determined a winner based on its standards.
Unofficial results Preliminary totals reported before certification.

The Associated Press says it declares a winner only when it determines there is no plausible scenario in which the trailing candidate could catch up. It does not require every ballot to be counted before calling a race.

When Will 2026 Midterm Election Results Be Announced?

There is no single time when all US midterm election results are announced.

Results begin appearing after polling places close and election officials begin releasing unofficial vote totals. Media organisations then analyse the reported numbers and may call individual races when the outcome becomes clear.

Some races may be called on Election Night. Others can take several days, particularly when the vote is close or when large numbers of mail ballots remain to be processed.

In previous elections, the AP did not call control of the Senate until four days after the 2022 midterms, while control of the House took eight days. In 2024, Senate control was called the following day, while the House took eight days. These are historical examples, not a prediction of the 2026 timetable.

What Are the Most Important Issues in the 2026 Midterms?

The election is not only about party control. Voters are also expressing their views on policy and the direction of the country.

  • Economy and cost of living: Prices, household budgets, housing costs, employment and economic conditions are important issues in national political coverage.
  • President Donald Trump's policies: The election is an important test of voter opinion about the President and the Republican administration.
  • Immigration and border policy: Immigration and border enforcement remain major points of disagreement between Republicans and Democrats.
  • Healthcare and reproductive rights: Healthcare costs, access to medical services and abortion policy are central issues in a number of state and national contests.
  • Foreign policy: Foreign policy, including the administration's approach to international conflicts, is part of the national political debate.
  • Redistricting and voting rules: Changes to congressional district boundaries and debates about election administration are also attracting attention in 2026.

Polls vs Voting Results: What's the Difference?

Many people search for terms such as "poll results today," "2026 polls" and "polls right now." These terms can refer to two different things.

Opinion polls ask a sample of people which candidate or party they support. They are conducted before or during a campaign to measure public opinion.

Voting results are based on actual ballots cast by eligible voters and counted by election officials.

A candidate leading in an opinion poll has not won an election. Likewise, a candidate leading in early vote returns may lose the lead as additional ballots are counted.

How Are Votes Counted in the United States?

The voting and counting process varies by state, but generally includes several steps.

  1. Ballot submission: Voters cast ballots in person, by mail or through an approved early voting process.
  2. Voter verification: Election officials check eligibility and follow the state's procedures.
  3. Ballot processing: Mail ballots and other ballots are prepared for counting under state rules.
  4. Vote tabulation: Voting machines or approved counting procedures record the votes.
  5. Unofficial reporting: Local election officials release preliminary totals.
  6. Race calls: News organisations assess the reported results and determine whether a winner can be called.
  7. Certification: Election authorities complete the formal canvass and certify the results according to state law.

A reported result can change as more ballots are processed. This is why an early lead should not automatically be treated as a victory.

Frequently Asked Questions About the 2026 Midterm Elections

1. When were the 2026 US midterm elections?
The 2026 US midterm elections took place on Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
2. What are midterm elections?
Midterm elections are elections held halfway through a US president's four-year term. They determine congressional seats and include many state and local elections.
3. Who is winning the 2026 midterm elections?
There is no single national winner. Senate, House and governor races are decided individually. Check the live results trackers for the latest reported counts and race calls.
4. When will the midterm election results be announced?
Some results can be reported on Election Night, while close races may take several days. The final certification process can take longer.
5. What time do polls close on Election Day?
Poll closing times vary by state and local jurisdiction. The first polling locations close at 6 p.m. ET, while the final locations in Alaska close at approximately 1 a.m. ET on Wednesday.
6. What time do polls close in Texas?
Most Texas polling locations close at 7 p.m. Central Time. Western Texas uses Mountain Time, so its polling locations close later in Eastern Time.
7. What are Americans voting for in 2026?
Americans are voting for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats, 36 governorships, state and local offices and various ballot measures.
8. Is Donald Trump running in the 2026 midterm elections?
No. Donald Trump is the sitting president, and the 2026 midterms are not a presidential election. His administration's policies are nevertheless major campaign issues.
9. How many Senate seats are up for election in 2026?
Thirty-five Senate seats are being contested, including regular and special elections.
10. How many House seats are being contested?
All 435 voting seats in the US House of Representatives are up for election.
11. Why are some election results delayed?
Results can be delayed because of close races, mail ballot processing, provisional ballots, recounts, local reporting schedules and ranked-choice voting.
12. Are election polls the same as voting results?
No. Opinion polls measure public preferences through surveys. Voting results are based on actual ballots cast and counted.
13. When will we know which party controls Congress?
Congressional control may not be known until several days after Election Day. The result depends on the number of House and Senate seats won by each party.
14. What is a blue wave in US elections?
A blue wave is an informal political term used when Democratic candidates achieve significant electoral gains. It is not an official election classification.
15. What is the Associated Press election result?
The Associated Press (AP) is a news organisation that independently analyses election returns and declares winners when its standards for calling a race are met. Its race calls are not the same as official state certification.

Final Thoughts: What to Watch in the 2026 Midterm Results

The 2026 United States midterm elections are an important test of political support for both Republicans and Democrats.

The main outcome to watch is control of Congress. Democrats are trying to gain enough seats to take control, while Republicans are seeking to retain their majorities. Senate battlegrounds, competitive House districts and gubernatorial contests will all contribute to the national picture.

The Texas Senate race between Ken Paxton and James Talarico is one of the contests attracting national attention. Meanwhile, voters across the country are deciding local offices and ballot measures that could shape state policies for years.

Although early results may appear quickly after polls close, the final picture can take days to emerge. Readers should follow verified election returns, distinguish a candidate's lead from a race call and wait for official certification before treating preliminary figures as final.
